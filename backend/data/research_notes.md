## 1. Statutory Provisions & Classification Bars

### 1.1 Patents Act, 1970 — Section 3(p)
* **Statutory Text**: *"an invention which in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components"* is not patentable.
* **Landmark Judicial Precedent**: *M/s The Zero Brand Zone Pvt. Ltd. v. The Controller of Patents & Designs* (Madras High Court, 2024):
  * First judicial definition of Traditional Knowledge in India (adopting WIPO/UNESCO definitions).
  * Held that even a single traditionally known component used for its known property triggers Section 3(p) refusal.
  * Held that the bar is **not absolute**: A patent can be granted if the applicant proves the formulation *"can no longer be described as being, in effect, traditional knowledge"*.
* **Overcoming Section 3(p)**:
  * Novel Drug Delivery Systems (NDDS): Liposomes, phytosomes, nano-emulsions, transdermal delivery.
  * Isolated active fractions / phytopharmaceuticals with standardized bioactive biomarkers not documented in classical texts.
  * Novel, non-obvious synergistic therapeutic indications backed by clinical and pharmacological data.

### 1.2 Patents Act, 1970 — Section 3(e) (The Mere Admixture Bar)
* **Statutory Text**: *"a substance obtained by a mere admixture resulting only in the aggregation of the properties of the components thereof, or a process for producing such substance"* is not patentable.
* **Legal Standard**: The applicant must prove **Synergistic Efficacy** — the combined technical effect is statistically significantly greater than the sum of individual components ($Effect(A+B) > Effect(A) + Effect(B)$).
* **Worked Example from 2025 IPO Guidelines**:
  * Tamarind Seed Polysaccharide (TSP): $40\text{ pg/ml}$
  * Helichrysum italicum extract (HIE): $21\text{ pg/ml}$
  * Expected additive: $61\text{ pg/ml}$
  * Actual combined measured: $140\text{ pg/ml} \implies$ **Synergistic $\implies$ Patentable**.

| **GP-1** | Extracts / alkaloids / isolated actives from a plant already known in AYUSH | **Product: NO**<br>**Process: YES** (if novel & inventive) | Sec 3(p), 3(c) | Novel extraction process yielding distinct therapeutic profile |
| **GP-2** | Combination of ingredients from plants/minerals already known for treating disease | **Presumed Obvious / Mere Admixture** | Sec 3(e), 3(p) | Specific stoichiometric ratios producing unexpected synergy |

## 3. Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940: The 4-Lane Regulatory Ladder

1. **Classical ASU Medicine**:
   * Manufactured strictly according to formulae in First Schedule authoritative texts (54 classical texts like *Charaka Samhita*, *Sushruta Samhita*, *Ashtanga Hridaya*).
   * No patent possible; no brand exclusivity. Proof of safety required; no clinical trials needed. Licensed by State Licensing Authorities (SLAs).
2. **Patent or Proprietary (P&P) Medicine (Rule 158B)**:
   * Formulations containing ingredients from classical texts with modified ratios, excipients, or delivery forms.
   * Safety backed by published textual rationale; pilot studies required only if indications depart from textual backing (Ministry of Ayush order dated 4 July 2018).
3. **Phytopharmaceutical Drug (Rule 2(eb), Nov 2015)**:
   * Standardized fraction with minimum 4 defined bioactive phytochemical markers.
   * Regulated under CDSCO as a New Drug (Appendix IB, Schedule Y). Full clinical trial data (Phase I–III) required. High patentability and export positioning.
4. **Ayurveda Aahara (FSSAI Regulations, May 2022)**:
   * Food prepared according to recipes/processes in Schedule A authoritative texts. Regulated under FSSAI (not CDSCO). Cannot claim disease treatment.

## 4. Biological Diversity Rules, 2024 & ABS Regulations, 2025

### 4.1 BD Rules 2024 Form Mapping (In Force 22 Dec 2024)
* **Form 1**: Prior approval to access biological resources for research by foreign entities ($₹10,000 / ₹20,000$).
* **Form 2**: Prior approval for commercial utilization by foreign entities ($₹10,000 / ₹20,000$).
* **Form 7**: Prior approval before grant of IPR for foreign applicants.
* **Form 8**: **Mandatory Registration before grant of IPR for Indian applicants** ($₹2,000 / ₹5,000$).
* **Form 9**: **Prior approval before commercialisation of IPR for Indian applicants** ($₹5,000 / ₹10,000$).
* **Form 11**: **Certificate of Origin for cultivated medicinal plants** from the BMC ($₹200$) to claim Section 7 exemption.
* **Mandatory Deadlines**: Applicant must notify NBA within **45 days** of IPR grant.

### 4.2 ABS Regulations 2025 Benefit-Sharing Slabs (Turnover Based)
* **Up to ₹5 Crore**: **EXEMPT** (Massive benefit for AYUSH MSMEs & startups).
* **₹5 Crore – ₹50 Crore**: **0.2%** of gross ex-factory sale price.
* **₹50 Crore – ₹250 Crore**: **0.4%**.
* **Above ₹250 Crore**: **0.6%**.
* **TK-IPR Uplift**: If an innovation is based on Traditional Knowledge and applicant applies for IPR, a **+25% surcharge** applies on the base rate.

## 5. Landmark Case Studies & Defenses

1. **Turmeric Patent (US 5,401,504)**:
   * Granted to University of Mississippi (1995) for wound healing. Revoked in 1997 after CSIR produced 32 ancient references in Sanskrit, Urdu, and Hindi. Directly sparked the creation of TKDL.
2. **Neem Patent (EP 436257)**:
   * Granted to USDA and W.R. Grace (1994) for antifungal neem oil. Revoked by EPO in 2000 for lack of inventive step over Indian traditional farming methods.
3. **Kani Tribe / Jeevani Model (1997)**:
   * TBGRI developed *Jeevani* from *Trichopus zeylanicus* (*Arogyapacha*). 50:50 license/royalty sharing with Kani tribe.
   * *Critical Lesson*: The lack of formal software auditing and supply chain tracking led to breakdown of the trust, underscoring why automated, transparent cryptographic ABS ledgers are required.
